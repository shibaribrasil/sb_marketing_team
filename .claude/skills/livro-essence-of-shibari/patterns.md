# Patterns & Techniques — Essence of Shibari

## Rope Treatment Protocol
**When to use**: With every new rope and after extended use.
**How**:
1. Burn off loose fibers with a quick flame pass (motion, not sustained heat).
2. Apply oil (camellia or similar) and work into rope by hand.
3. Run rope repeatedly around a smooth post or between hands under tension to soften fibers.
4. Hang to air dry completely.
5. Repeat steps 1–2 as fibers re-emerge with use.
**Trade-offs**: Time-intensive but non-negotiable; untreated rope is abrasive, uncontrollable, and harder on skin.

## Single Column Tie (SCT) — Square Knot Variant
**When to use**: Standard single-limb tie; fastest of the three variants.
**How**: Cross rope over body part → wrap once → pull head back under → cross over → tuck through → pull firm. Maintain gap.
**Trade-offs**: Requires accurate force calibration; harder for beginners to feel the right tension.

## Single Column Tie — Double Square Knot Variant
**When to use**: When firmness matters more than speed; better for learners.
**How**: Same as Square Knot SCT but adds a second crossing before tucking.
**Trade-offs**: More steps; easier to achieve consistent firmness.

## Single Column Tie — Carrick Bend Variant
**When to use**: When firmness + speed are both priorities AND the wrap does not need to be tight.
**How**: Carrick bend structure instead of square knot finish.
**Trade-offs**: Cannot accommodate situations where wraps need to be relatively tight — use Square Knot SCT in those cases.

---

## Double Column Tie
**When to use**: Tying two column-shaped parts together (e.g., wrists).
**How**: Apply SCT foundation to both columns simultaneously; connect without closing gap on either.
**Trade-offs**: Builds from SCT — master SCT first.

---

## Figure 8 Friction
**When to use**: Holding rope in place mid-run without a permanent knot.
**How**: Wrap working end around rope stem in a figure-8 pattern; tension holds without tying.
**Trade-offs**: Adjustable and releasable; less secure than a knot under very high tension.

---

## Double Half Hitch (Finishing Knot)
**When to use**: Standard finish for any tie.
**How**: Loop working end over rope run → through → repeat → pull firm.
**Trade-offs**: Secure and cleanly releasable — the correct default finish.

---

## One Rope Technique
**When to use**: Opening sessions with a new partner; restoring presence when feeling mechanical; teaching the relational foundation.
**How**: Take one rope only. No knots. No connections. Use rope to touch, wrap, and feel — following the partner's body. End with same physical closeness as the start.
**Trade-offs**: Provides no restriction; its purpose is relational attunement, not bondage.

## TK (Takate Kote) — Nerve-Safe Execution
**When to use**: Any TK tie, regardless of experience level.
**How** (nerve-protection sequence):
1. Position bottom: both hands behind back, forearms parallel, wrists facing opposite directions. Shoulders relaxed.
2. Pass rope head upward between arms and torso; pull to neck height.
3. Wrap once around both arms.
4. Drop rope head; fold working section back; set fast bowline (loop around thumb).
5. Cross rope head over all ropes, then under; pass through loop; pull firm.
6. Check: two finger-widths gap in forearm tie; bottom can exchange hand positions.
7. Wrap upper arm (clockwise over left); second wrap goes below first.
8. Cross rope at left shoulder; hook from inside out; insert two fingers into rope ring; adjust for even tension; ensure ropes lay flat.
**Trade-offs**: More deliberate than speed-optimized TK; prevents radial nerve injury which causes drop wrist.

## Pre-Session Assessment (Rope Top Protocol)
**When to use**: Before every session, especially with a new partner or after a long break.
**How**:
1. Discuss experience level, flexibility, and endurance.
2. Disclose tie type and expected discomfort.
3. Establish safe word.
4. Assess existing injuries.
5. Check room temperature.
**Trade-offs**: Takes 5–10 minutes; prevents most avoidable injuries and consent violations.

## Rope-to-Person Priority (Furniture Application)
**When to use**: Every time a fixture is involved in the scene.
**How**: Tie the body first. Hook or anchor existing body rope to the fixture — never wrap the fixture first and work toward the person.
**Trade-offs**: Requires thinking rope-end first rather than fixture-first; produces cleaner tension and more intentional bondage.

---

## Partial Suspension Setup
**When to use**: When a suspension point is available but full suspension is not appropriate.
**How**:
1. Apply ground-based tie (TK or harness) to bottom first.
2. Route a rope from the body harness upward to the suspension point.
3. Adjust tension to create position/pull without fully lifting bottom off the ground.
4. Inspect carabiner rating before use.
**Trade-offs**: Accessible without full suspension skill; introduces load that multiplies all safety considerations.

---

## Semenawa Calibration
**When to use**: D/s domination sessions where controlled suffering is the intent.
**How**:
- Control three variables in real time: *location* of suffering, *intensity* (tightness, position), and *tempo* (rhythm of tightening and release).
- Calibrate continuously based on the bottom's state — never pre-plan the full arc.
- Maintain constant observation; stop immediately at signs outside the agreed range.
**Trade-offs**: Requires advanced relational attunement (Ch3) and safety knowledge (Ch5) — not a technique for beginners.

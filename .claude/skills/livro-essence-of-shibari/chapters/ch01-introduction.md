# Chapter 1: Introduction

## Core Idea
Kinbaku serves multiple purposes — intimate play, semenawa, photography, and stage performance — and each demands a different focus. Before picking up a rope, know which one you are practicing.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Purpose-First Study**: Define the goal of your bondage before choosing techniques. The direction of study and the specific skills prioritized differ entirely by context.
  - When to use: At the beginning of every practice session and when teaching beginners.
  - How: Ask "Is this for intimacy, domination, aesthetics, or performance?" Then weight technique, safety knowledge, tempo, and showmanship accordingly.

- **The Four Contexts of Kinbaku**:
  1. *Intimate play* — closeness over technique; minimal skill required, maximum presence.
  2. *Semenawa* (domination through suffering) — requires deep safety knowledge and command of time and tempo.
  3. *Photography* — attention to detail; no need for theatrical speed.
  4. *Stage performance* — speed, force, and sightlines; push the extremes while balancing model comfort.

## Key Concepts
- **Kinbaku / Shibari**: Used interchangeably historically; in modern English usage, *shibari* often refers to Westernized aesthetic ropework, *kinbaku* to the Japanese style. Author uses both.
- **Semenawa**: Suffering within a controlled range by rope — the domination application of kinbaku. Requires heightened safety awareness.
- **Nawashi / Bakushi**: Rope bondage master, teacher, or artist. The rope top with developed expertise.
- **Hojojutsu**: Edo-period Japanese martial art of tying prisoners — the historical root of modern kinbaku. Over 150 schools developed between 1603–1868.
- **Kitan Club**: Pioneering Japanese SM magazine from the 1950s that helped establish kinbaku as a public art form.
- **Ito Seiu**: Pioneer of kinbaku as erotic art; his work in early 20th century Japan transformed hojojutsu aesthetics into a sexual art form.

## Mental Models
- Use purpose-first thinking when selecting which chapters of this book to study deeply vs. reference.
- Think of bakushi as cultural authorities: kinbaku in Japan evolved through their influence, not through formal institutions — study lineage to understand style.
- Western kinbaku has caught up to Japanese quality in the past decade; proximity to Japan gave Chinese-speaking regions earlier exposure but slower development.

## Key Takeaways
1. Define your purpose before choosing techniques — intimate play, semenawa, photography, and performance each require different priorities.
2. Kinbaku's roots are in hojojutsu (Edo-period martial restraint) and theatrical torture scenes (kabuki/Soushi Shibai) — both lineages shaped the aesthetics.
3. The book is designed to serve all levels: beginners wanting safe basics, intermediate practitioners wanting common ties, and advanced students wanting TK and practical applications.
4. Consent is a foundational tenet: informed, active consent between adults is required before any practice.

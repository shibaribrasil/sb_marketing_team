# Chapter 5: Safety Issues

## Core Idea
Safety in kinbaku is a system, not a checklist. It requires anatomical knowledge (where nerves and vessels are shallow), pre-session communication, active monitoring during the session, and a defined emergency response. The rope top is fully responsible for the rope bottom's safety once the scene begins.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Tie Zones vs. Avoid Zones**: The general rule — tie over thick muscle and bone; avoid shallow nerves and vessels. Memorize the specific zones before tying.
  - When to use: Before every session, especially when adapting ties to a new body.
  - How: See reference table below.

- **Pre-Session Assessment Protocol** (Rope Top responsibility):
  1. Discuss experience level, flexibility, and endurance.
  2. Disclose what bondage will happen and possible discomfort.
  3. Establish safe word (especially for D/s play).
  4. Assess any existing injuries or medical conditions.
  5. Check temperature of space — cold reduces bottom's flexibility and endurance.

- **Active Monitoring During Session**: Constantly observe the rope bottom for warning signs — numbness, tingling, aches, unusual color change in extremities. These are not complaints to manage — they are signals to act on immediately.

- **Nerve Compression Response**: Numbness or tingling is a nerve under compression. Stop. Release the relevant wraps. Do not continue. Assess before deciding to re-tie.

- **Rope Bottom's Self-Responsibilities**:
  - Choose partners carefully — verify experience and trust before allowing yourself to be tied.
  - Know your own limits and communicate them before the session.
  - Understand that models and performers have specific responsibilities different from private play.

## Key Concepts
- **Rope Bottom / Rope Top**: Bottom is the person being tied; Top is the person tying. Top holds safety responsibility during the scene.
- **Safe word**: A pre-agreed signal that stops play immediately. Required for D/s contexts.
- **Radial nerve**: Runs along the outer forearm; at particular risk in TK and arm binder ties.
- **Brachial plexus**: Nerve network running through the armpit; direct compression causes rapid numbness to the arm.
- **Carotid artery**: Runs on both sides of the neck; compression causes oxygen deprivation to the brain within seconds. Neck is an absolute avoid zone.
- **Breath play**: Author explicitly states this is high-risk even for experienced players — among the leading causes of BDSM-related fatalities. Avoid.
- **EMT scissors (safety shears)**: Required equipment for rope tops — the standard emergency cutting tool.

## Reference Table: Body Zones

| Zone | Rule | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Wrist (inner groove) | Avoid — tie to outer/bony edge | Radial/ulnar vessels and nerves are shallow |
| Inside of elbow | Avoid | Shallow nerves and blood vessels |
| Armpit | Never constrict directly | Brachial plexus — numbness is immediate |
| Ankle (lateral tip) | Avoid directly; use wide SCT adjusted to lower leg | Fragile bone prominence |
| Waist / hip | Tie narrower hip area for security; hip bones for suspension | Hip bones support weight without compressing organs |
| Neck (carotid sides) | Absolute avoid | 10 seconds to loss of consciousness from carotid compression |
| Windpipe | Absolute avoid | Impedes breathing even with light pressure |
| Thigh / upper arm | Safe tie zones | Thick muscle, bones protect vessels |
| Rib area | Tie over ribs, not between | Ribs protect chest organs |

## Anti-patterns
- **Leaving the rope bottom unattended once tied**: The top must remain present at all times.
- **Assuming "they said it was okay before" applies now**: Active consent is session-by-session and moment-by-moment.
- **Ignoring reported numbness or tingling**: These are nerve compression signals — not normal discomfort to push through.
- **Moving the rope bottom (turning them) instead of moving yourself around them**: Causes dizziness and disorientation.
- **Playing in extreme cold without adjusting**: Cold reduces flexibility and endurance — what worked last session may cause injury today.
- **Trusting ornamental carabiners for suspension**: Only use carabiners with certified safety ratings for load-bearing.

## Key Takeaways
1. Tie over thick muscle and bone; avoid zones where nerves and vessels are shallow — especially the inner wrist groove, inside of elbow, armpit, and neck.
2. The neck is an absolute avoid zone — carotid compression causes loss of consciousness in ~10 seconds with an unpredictably small safety margin.
3. Pre-session assessment is the top's responsibility: experience, injuries, flexibility, safe word, and what to expect from the upcoming tie.
4. Numbness or tingling = nerve compression = stop and release. It is never a signal to continue.
5. The top must remain present for the entire scene. The bottom has entrusted their safety to you.

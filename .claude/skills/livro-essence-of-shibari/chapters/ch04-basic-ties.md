# Chapter 4: Basic Ties

## Core Idea
The single column tie is the entire foundation of kinbaku. Every other tie is built from it. Mastering its three variants — with their trade-offs of speed, firmness, and tightness tolerance — is the prerequisite for everything in Chapters 6 and 7.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Single Column Tie (三種)**: Three variants serve different needs. Choose by context, not by habit.
  - *Square Knot SCT*: Fewest steps; requires accurate force calibration — harder for beginners.
  - *Double Square Knot SCT*: More steps; easier to make firm — good for learners.
  - *Carrick Bend SCT*: Fewest steps + firmest hold; not suitable where wraps need to be tight.
  - When to use: SCT for any column-shaped body part (wrist, ankle, thigh, waist). Choose variant based on load and flexibility needs.
  - How: Multiple wraps forming a flat, even surface; maintain a gap between wraps and skin for blood flow. This gap principle is the central tenet of kinbaku — never compress completely.

- **Double Column Tie**: Ties two columns together (e.g., both wrists). Builds from SCT; adds connection between columns without closing circulation.

- **Friction System**: How ropes are held in place without knots. Frictions are preferable to knots in many positions because they are adjustable and release more cleanly.
  - *Figure 8 Friction*: Most common; versatile anchor point.
  - *Cross Friction*: Variation for different angles.
  - *Twisted Cross Friction*: Added security, more complex to release.

- **Half Hitch / Double Half Hitch**: Used to lock and finish ties. Double half hitch is the standard finishing knot — secure and releasable.

- **Rope Connection Methods**:
  - *Lark's Head*: Quick, reversible — use to add rope to a bight or ring.
  - *Square Knot*: Joins two rope ends; strong but can jam under load.
  - *Sheet Bend*: Joins ropes of different diameters; preferred when ropes don't match.

- **Lock Stitch Crocheting**: A decorative and functional technique for creating patterns along rope runs — used for aesthetic chest harnesses and rhombus patterns.

- **Improvisation (Comprehensive Exercise)**: Nawakiri's principle: once basics are internalized, improvisation should feel natural — not planned. The test of a basic tie is whether it can be applied spontaneously.

## Key Concepts
- **Gap principle**: Every wrap must maintain a gap between rope and skin to allow blood flow — the rope compresses against itself, not the body.
- **Flat, even surface**: Wraps should lay parallel and flat — crossing or twisting wraps create uneven pressure points.
- **Rope head section**: Used to start the wrap; the working end executes the knot.
- **Bight**: A curve or loop in the rope that hasn't crossed itself — used as an anchor point in many frictions.
- **Working end / tail**: The active end of the rope being manipulated to form the tie.

## Reference Table: Basic Tie Selection

| Situation | Recommended Tie | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Single limb, general | SCT (Square Knot) | Fast, standard |
| Single limb, beginner top | SCT (Double Square Knot) | Easier to make firm |
| Single limb, tight wrap needed | SCT (Carrick Bend) — NOT suitable | Use Square Knot SCT instead |
| Two limbs together | Double Column Tie | Connects without compressing |
| Holding rope in place mid-run | Figure 8 Friction | Adjustable, releasable |
| Finishing a tie | Double Half Hitch | Secure, standard finish |
| Adding rope (same diameter) | Lark's Head or Square Knot | Quick connection |
| Adding rope (different diameter) | Sheet Bend | Compensates for mismatch |

## Anti-patterns
- **Crossing wraps**: Creates uneven pressure — one side bears all the load.
- **Zero-gap wraps**: Even a "snug" wrap with no gap restricts circulation. Always maintain gap.
- **Using Carrick Bend SCT where wraps need to be tight**: It can't accommodate tight wrap requirements — use Square Knot SCT instead.
- **Rushing improvisation**: Lock Stitch and improvisation exercises come after the three SCT variants are muscle memory, not alongside them.

## Key Takeaways
1. The single column tie — in all three variants — is the foundation of every tie in this book. Master it before anything else.
2. The gap principle is the central safety tenet: multiple flat wraps, always with space for circulation.
3. Frictions (Figure 8, Cross, Twisted Cross) are preferable to knots mid-run — adjustable and cleaner to release.
4. Rope connection method depends on diameter match: Lark's Head or Square Knot for same, Sheet Bend for different.
5. Improvisation is the test of internalized basics — if you have to think about SCT, it isn't learned yet.

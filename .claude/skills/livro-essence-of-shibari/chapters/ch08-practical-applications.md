# Chapter 8: Practical Applications

## Core Idea
Furniture, suspension points, and semenawa are not advanced stages — they are contexts that reveal whether your foundational knowledge is solid. Nawakiri's principle: always direct the rope toward the person, not toward the furniture.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Rope-to-Person Priority**: When using furniture or suspension points, attach rope to the bottom's body first; use the fixture only to redirect or anchor existing rope. Do not spend rope on furniture — spend it on the person.
  - When to use: Every time a fixture (chair, table, suspension ring) is involved.
  - How: Start with a tie on the body. Hook or anchor that rope to the fixture — don't wrap the fixture first and then work to the person.

- **Partial Suspension Framework**: Full suspension is not the goal of this chapter. Nawakiri deliberately limits scope to partial suspension — using a suspension point to create unusual positions and tension without lifting the bottom fully off the ground.
  - When to use: When a suspension point is available but full suspension is not appropriate (skill, equipment, or safety reasons).
  - How: Ground the bottom; use the suspension point to extend a rope run upward, creating tension and position control without bearing full weight.

- **Semenawa Principles**:
  - Semenawa is suffering within a controlled range — the nawashi controls *how much* discomfort, *where*, and *for how long*.
  - Tempo is everything: the nawashi controls the rhythm of tightening, positioning, and release to shape the bottom's experience.
  - It requires active, real-time calibration — a semenawa session cannot be planned entirely in advance; it responds to the bottom's state moment by moment.

## Key Concepts
- **Semenawa**: Rope suffering/torture within a controlled range — the domination application of kinbaku. Distinct from aesthetic or intimate kinbaku.
- **Partial suspension**: Using a suspension point to create tension or unusual body position without fully lifting the bottom off the ground.
- **Full suspension**: The bottom's full body weight is borne by the rope — explicitly outside the scope of this book.
- **Carabiner (certified)**: Must carry a certified load rating for suspension use. Ornamental carabiners cannot bear weight — a critical safety distinction.
- **Door frames / clothes stands**: Cannot bear suspension weight — common mistakes for improvised setups.
- **Nawashi's spatial awareness**: On-stage performance requires constant awareness of sightlines — step away from the bottom so the audience can see; do not block the scene.

## Reference Table: Furniture Applications

| Fixture | First action | Key principle |
|---|---|---|
| Chair | Tie wrist with thigh-tie-like method, affix to chair back | Hook working end to body rope, not furniture |
| Table | Single column tie on wrists, fasten to table leg | Stretch limbs to bottom's flexibility limit; use leftover rope on thigh, not table |
| Suspension ring | Tie body first (TK or harness), route rope to ring | Partial suspension — body still partly grounded |

## Anti-patterns
- **Wrapping furniture and then working to the person**: Wastes rope on the fixture, reduces what reaches the body, and creates sloppy tension paths.
- **Trusting equipment without certification**: Door frames, clothes stands, and decorative carabiners are not tested for load. Only use purpose-made suspension hardware with rated specifications.
- **Treating semenawa as just "tighter bondage"**: Semenawa is a relational and temporal art — the suffering is calibrated and controlled. Without real-time monitoring and calibration, it becomes simple disregard for the bottom.
- **Attempting full suspension from this chapter**: Nawakiri explicitly excludes full suspension from scope — Ch8's partial suspension is a stepping stone, not the destination.

## Key Takeaways
1. Rope goes to the person, not to the furniture — fixtures redirect or anchor, they don't receive.
2. Full suspension is outside this book's scope. Partial suspension is accessible; it uses suspension points for position and tension without full weight-bearing.
3. Semenawa requires real-time calibration — tempo, location of suffering, and duration are the nawashi's active tools, not pre-planned variables.
4. All suspension hardware must have certified load ratings. Ornamental carabiners, door frames, and clothes stands cannot bear weight.
5. Nawakiri's principle: "Devote your attention to the person you are tying instead of the rope or the furniture."

# Chapter 7: Takate Kote (TK) Shibari Tie

## Core Idea
TK is the most photographed and performed tie in kinbaku — and the highest-risk. Its difficulty is not the technique but the nerve management: the radial nerve runs directly through the zone the tie occupies. Executing TK correctly means building nerve protection into each step, not adding it at the end.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **TK Nerve Prevention Protocol**: Nerve protection is built into the tie's steps, not a post-tie check.
  - Step 1: Forearms parallel with wrists facing each other — prevents nerve compression at the wrist from the start.
  - Gap requirement: At least two finger-widths between single column tie and forearms — allows the bottom to shift hand position.
  - Wrap alignment: Second wrap must go *below* the first — consistent structure prevents uneven load.
  - Final chest pass: Adjust for even tension before securing.

- **Fast Bowline (Wykd Dave variant)**: An SCT variant used specifically for the forearms in TK. Simple, fast hand movement, but the tail can pull straight under strong tension — sufficient for forearm use, not for load-bearing suspension alone.
  - When to use: As the forearm tie in TK standard form.

- **TK Decoration Framework**: The basic form produces a clean, functional tie. Decorations (V Neck, Akechi Denki style) add aesthetic complexity and should only be applied when the basic form is already consistent.

- **Ebi Tie and Reverse Ebi**: Position ties derived from TK.
  - *Ebi (Shrimp)*: Body folded forward — knees toward chest. High physical demand; increases pressure on the chest from TK.
  - *Reverse Ebi*: Body bent backward — extreme arch. Among the most demanding positions; significant spinal stress.
  - *Agura (seated)*: Cross-legged base position for a less extreme Ebi variant.

## Key Concepts
- **Takate Kote (TK)**: "High hand small" — both arms bound behind the back with a structured chest harness. The globally standard kinbaku chest harness.
- **Akechi Denki**: The most influential bakushi in the modern history of kinbaku; the TK variant in this book follows his and Kanna's lineage.
- **Rope stem / rope spine**: The center vertical run of rope from which wraps are built in TK — the structural axis of the tie.
- **Radial nerve**: Runs along the outer upper arm; TK's wraps pass directly adjacent to it. Compression here causes "drop wrist" — a serious injury.
- **Ebi (shrimp)**: Forward fold position derived from TK. Increases TK chest pressure.
- **Reverse Ebi**: Backward arch derived from TK. Extremely demanding; spinal and shoulder stress.
- **V Neck decoration**: A rope run creating a V pattern at the front of the chest — a common TK embellishment.
- **Ushoro Takete Tasuki-Gake (Akechi Denki style)**: A specific diagonal rope decoration associated with the Akechi school.

## Anti-patterns
- **Zero gap between forearm tie and skin**: The bottom must be able to exchange hand positions during the tie — two finger-widths minimum is not optional.
- **First wrap above second wrap**: Consistent wrap order (second below first) is structural — reversing it changes load distribution.
- **Attempting Ebi or Reverse Ebi before TK basic form is consistent**: These position modifiers amplify every flaw in the base tie.
- **Using TK as a beginner's tie because it looks simple in photos**: TK is intermediate/advanced. The nerve risk is high and the technique requires precision that beginners haven't yet developed.
- **Skipping 7.1.3 (Nerve Compression Prevention) for a fast tie**: This subsection is not optional detail — it's the safety core of TK.

## Worked Example
*TK basic form — nerve-protection sequence:*
1. Have the bottom place both hands behind their back, forearms parallel, wrists facing opposite directions (not crossed). Shoulders relaxed.
2. Pass rope head upward through the space between arms and torso; pull to neck height.
3. Wrap once around both arms.
4. Drop rope head down; fold working section back on itself (fast bowline setup). Thumb placement controls the loop.
5. Cross rope head over all ropes, then under — loop forms around thumb.
6. Pass rope head through loop. Pull firm.
7. *Check*: Can the bottom exchange hand positions? Two-finger gap present? If not, release and reset.
8. Wrap rope over the upper arm (clockwise over left arm in example). Second wrap goes *below* first.
9. Cross rope on left shoulder; stand behind bottom; hook rope from inside out with dominant hand.
10. Insert index and middle fingers of non-dominant hand into rope ring — adjust for even tension, ensure ropes lay flat before securing.

## Key Takeaways
1. TK is the most photographed and most dangerous common tie — nerve management is built into each step, not added afterward.
2. The radial nerve runs through TK's primary wrap zone — "drop wrist" (radial nerve injury) is the signature TK injury.
3. Minimum two finger-widths of gap in the forearm tie is non-negotiable — the bottom must be able to shift hand position.
4. The basic form must be consistent before any decoration (V Neck, Akechi style) is attempted.
5. Ebi and Reverse Ebi are extreme position modifiers — they amplify TK's pressure and stress, not reduce it.

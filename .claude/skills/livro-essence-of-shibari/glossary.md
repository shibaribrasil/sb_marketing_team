# Glossary — Essence of Shibari

**Agura** — Cross-legged seated position; used as base for the less extreme Ebi variation. (Ch7)

**Akechi Denki** — Most influential bakushi in modern kinbaku; his style is referenced in TK decoration and hip/crotch tie variants. (Ch6, Ch7)

**Arm Binder** — Both arms bound behind the back — high restriction, significant shoulder stress. Precursor to Strappado. (Ch6)

**Asanawa** — Traditional Japanese rope twist method; the standard construction for kinbaku hemp/jute rope. (Ch2)

**Bakushi** — Rope bondage master, teacher, or artist. The culturally influential figures who shaped kinbaku aesthetics. (Ch1)

**Bight** — A curve or loop in the rope that hasn't crossed itself — used as anchor point in frictions and knots. (Ch4)

**Brachial plexus** — Nerve network running through the armpit; direct compression causes rapid arm numbness. Absolute avoid zone. (Ch5)

**Breath play** — Any activity that restricts breathing for effect. Author explicitly marks this as extremely high-risk, even for experienced players. (Ch5)

**Carrick Bend SCT** — Single column tie variant: fewest steps + firmest hold, but not usable where wraps need to be tight. (Ch4)

**Carotid artery** — Blood vessel on both sides of the neck. Compression causes loss of consciousness in ~10 seconds. Neck is absolute avoid zone. (Ch5)

**D/s (Dominance/submission)** — Power-exchange dynamic between partners; requires pre-negotiated safe word in kinbaku contexts. (Ch5)

**Double Column Tie** — Ties two column-shaped body parts together (e.g., both wrists). Built from SCT. (Ch4)

**Double Half Hitch** — Standard finishing knot in kinbaku — secure and cleanly releasable. (Ch4)

**Ebi** — "Shrimp" tie — body folded forward, knees toward chest. Derived from TK; increases chest pressure. (Ch7)

**EMT scissors / Safety shears** — Recommended emergency cutting tool for rope tops; required equipment. (Ch5)

**Fast Bowline** (Wykd Dave variant) — SCT variant used for TK forearm tie; simple, fast, but tail can pull straight under high tension. (Ch7)

**Figure 8 Friction** — Most common friction knot; versatile anchor point, adjustable and releasable. (Ch4)

**Friction** — A rope-holding technique (not a knot) that keeps rope in place while remaining adjustable. Preferred over knots mid-run. (Ch4)

**Futomomo** — Thigh tie — ankle bound to thigh, creating a compact folded-leg position. (Ch6)

**Gap principle** — Every wrap must maintain a gap between rope and skin for blood circulation; wraps compress against themselves, never the body. Central safety tenet of kinbaku. (Ch4, Ch5)

**Half Hitch** — Basic locking knot; Double Half Hitch is the standard finish for most ties. (Ch4)

**Hemp rope** — Collective term for natural fiber ropes used in kinbaku; includes jute (Japan standard), linen, and others. (Ch2)

**Hojojutsu** — Edo-period Japanese martial art of tying prisoners; 150+ schools developed 1603–1868; the historical root of modern kinbaku. (Ch1)

**Jute** — Most commonly used fiber in Japanese kinbaku. Rough texture for friction knots, low elasticity for tactile control. (Ch2)

**Kame Shibari** — Tortoise Shell Tie; full-body diamond rope pattern, the most visually iconic kinbaku style. (Ch6)

**Kinbaku** — Japanese rope bondage; used interchangeably with shibari, though in modern English usage kinbaku often refers specifically to the Japanese style. (Ch1)

**Kitan Club** — Pioneering Japanese SM magazine from the 1950s; early public platform for kinbaku as art. (Ch1)

**Lark's Head** — Quick rope connection method; reversible; used to add rope to a bight or ring. (Ch4)

**Lock Stitch Crocheting** — Decorative and functional pattern technique; required for Tortoise Shell / Rhombus Rope. (Ch4)

**Nawashi** — Rope artist; typically refers to a skilled kinbaku top with developed expertise. (Ch1)

**Nawashi Kanna** — Nawakiri's teacher; founder of the Kanna school of kinbaku; wrote the foreword to this book. (Ch1, Ch7)

**One Rope technique** — Philosophy of using a single rope with no knots; strips away technique to restore focus on the partner. (Ch3)

**Partial suspension** — Using a suspension point to create tension and unusual positions without bearing the bottom's full body weight. Within the scope of this book; full suspension is not. (Ch8)

**Radial nerve** — Runs along the outer upper arm/forearm; TK's primary compression risk. Injury causes "drop wrist." (Ch5, Ch7)

**Reverse Ebi** — Backward arch position derived from TK; extreme spinal and shoulder stress. (Ch7)

**Rope Bottom** — The person being tied. Has active responsibilities: partner selection, self-knowledge, communication. (Ch5)

**Rope Top** — The person tying. Holds safety responsibility during the session. (Ch5)

**Safe word** — Pre-agreed signal that stops play immediately. Required for D/s contexts. (Ch5)

**Semenawa** — Rope suffering within a controlled range; the domination/torture application of kinbaku. Requires real-time calibration of tempo, location, and duration. (Ch1, Ch8)

**Sheet Bend** — Rope connection method for joining ropes of different diameters. (Ch4)

**Shime Nawa** — Sacred rope used to cordon off Shinto shrines; illustrates rope's deep cultural role in Japan. (Ch1)

**Shibari** — Japanese rope bondage; in modern English often refers to Westernized aesthetic ropework. Used interchangeably with kinbaku in this book. (Ch1)

**Single Column Tie (SCT)** — The foundational kinbaku tie for any column-shaped body part. Three variants: Square Knot, Double Square Knot, Carrick Bend. (Ch4)

**Square Knot SCT** — SCT variant with fewest steps; requires accurate force calibration, harder for beginners. (Ch4)

**Strappado** — Arms bound behind and pulled upward — very high tension on shoulders. (Ch6)

**Takate Kote (TK)** — "High hand small" — the globally standard kinbaku chest harness; arms behind back with structured wraps. Most photographed and highest-risk common tie. (Ch7)

**Tortoise Shell Tie** — See Kame Shibari. (Ch6)

**Ushoro Takete Tasuki-Gake** — Akechi Denki-style diagonal rope decoration for TK. (Ch7)

**V Neck** — A common TK decoration creating a V pattern at the front of the chest. (Ch7)

**Wykd Dave** — British nawashi who popularized the Fast Bowline for TK forearm tie; also credited with the One Rope philosophy. (Ch3, Ch7)

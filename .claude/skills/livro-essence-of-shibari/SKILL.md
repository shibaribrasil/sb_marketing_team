## Core Frameworks & Mental Models

### Purpose-First Study
Define the goal before choosing any technique. Each purpose demands a different skill set:
- **Intimate play** → closeness over technique; Ch3 (One Rope) is the primary reference
- **Semenawa** → safety depth + real-time calibration of tempo, location, and intensity
- **Photography** → attention to detail; speed is secondary
- **Stage performance** → speed, force, and sightline management; push extremes within model safety

*Use this framework at the start of every practice session and when teaching beginners.*

---

### The Gap Principle (Central Safety Tenet)
Every wrap must maintain a gap between rope and skin. Wraps compress against each other — never against the body. Multiple flat, parallel wraps create a stable surface; no crossing, no zero-gap compression.

*This is non-negotiable in every tie. "Snug" without a gap is too tight.*

---

### Material Selection Rules
| Material | Best use | Key trade-off |
|---|---|---|
| Jute (6mm × 7.5–8m) | Standard kinbaku | Rough, inelastic, friction-ready |
| Cotton | Casual, comfort-first | Elastic — knots jam under tension |
| Nylon | Wet environments | Smooth surface changes technique |
| 8mm jute | Larger body types | Same properties, bigger size |
| Manila/Sisal | Never | Too rough to soften for skin |

**Treatment Protocol**: New rope → flame (burn fibers) → oil → work → dry. Repeat as needed.

---

### Tie Zones vs. Avoid Zones
**Tie over**: thick muscle (upper arm, thigh), bone (ribs, hip bones for suspension), lower leg (adjusted wide SCT at ankle)

**Absolute avoid**: neck (both sides = carotid; center = windpipe), armpit (brachial plexus), inner wrist groove, inside of elbow

**Warning signals during session**: Numbness or tingling = nerve compression = stop and release immediately.

---

### Nerve Warning → Response
| Signal | Action |
|---|---|
| Numbness / tingling | Stop. Release relevant wraps. Assess before proceeding. |
| "Drop wrist" (TK) | Stop immediately. Medical attention. Radial nerve injury. |
| Unusual color (blue/white) | Release. Do not re-tie same zone. |

---

### TK Nerve-Protection Protocol
TK has the highest nerve risk of any common tie. Nerve protection is built into each step:
1. Forearms parallel, wrists facing opposite directions — prevents wrist nerve compression
2. Minimum two finger-widths gap in forearm tie — bottom must be able to exchange hand positions
3. Second upper arm wrap goes below first — consistent load distribution
4. Adjust for even tension across chest before securing

*Skipping 7.1.3 (Nerve Compression Prevention) is the most common TK mistake.*

---

### "Seeing the Rope, Missing the Person"
Technique focus creates a bottleneck that disconnects top from bottom. The cure: deliberate return to original intention. One Rope (Wykd Dave / Hedwig Ve): one rope, no knots, attention entirely on the partner.

*Apply when feeling mechanical, when a bottom reports feeling like an object, or at the start of any new practice relationship.*

---

### Rope-to-Person Priority (Furniture/Suspension)
When using fixtures: tie the body first, then anchor rope to the fixture. Never wrap the fixture first and work toward the person. Spend rope on the person, not on the furniture.

---

### Semenawa Calibration
Three variables to control in real time: **location** of suffering, **intensity** (tightness, position), **tempo** (rhythm of tightening and release). Cannot be fully pre-planned — must respond to the bottom's state moment by moment.
